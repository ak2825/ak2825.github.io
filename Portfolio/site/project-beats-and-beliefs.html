Project case study

Beats and Beliefs

Exploring how political freedom, language, and global influence appear in popular-music charts.

Anna Kim · Python · R · SQL

GitHub repository link coming soon
1Context 2Approach 3Findings 4What I learned

Track 1

Context

I compared Spotify Top 50 chart data with country-level democracy indices to ask how political freedom might relate to the music people encounter in national charts.

Track 2

Approach

I used SQL to prepare and connect chart records, then Python and R to explore the data and run multivariate regression. The analysis treated language and global reach as important context rather than reading one variable in isolation.

Track 3

Findings

The chart comparisons raised a broader interpretation problem: a view labeled “global” can still reflect the outsized reach of English-language music and obscure local listening contexts.

That globalization bias mattered to how I read the regression. The statistical relationship was only one part of the story; the structure of the charts and the cultures represented in them shaped what the model could mean.

Track 4

What I learned

Multivariate regression can help separate overlapping explanations, but it does not remove the need to examine how a dataset was assembled and whose behavior it represents.

I learned to treat visualization choices as analytical choices. A clear chart should reveal geographic and language patterns without turning a limited sample into a universal claim.
